Club and team dues are small amounts of money that generate a wildly disproportionate amount of administration.

The reason is straightforward: **the work isn't collecting the money, it's keeping track of who hasn't paid.**

## 📝 Chasing, not collecting, is the job

Say a squad of 24 pays $16 a month. Collecting is a few days' work whether it's bank transfers or cash.

What doesn't finish is the three people who haven't paid. Cross-checking the payment list against the membership list, messaging the likely candidates individually, messaging again when nobody replies. That happens every month. The sum involved is $48 and the time involved is nothing like $48's worth.

There is only one way to make this lighter. **Reduce the number of occasions on which someone can fail to pay.**

## 🧮 Collections multiply into things to chase

If the annual costs are fixed, the yearly figure per member is the same however you collect it. What changes is how many opportunities there are to fall behind.

Annual fixed costs of $4,800 across 24 members is $200 each per year. Three ways to collect it:

| Method | Per payment | Collections a year | Chances to go unpaid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monthly | ~$17 | 12 | 288 |
| Quarterly | $50 | 4 | 96 |
| Annually | $200 | 1 | 24 |

Monthly is gentlest on members and inflates the treasurer's tracking to 288 events. Annual keeps tracking down to 24 and asks a brand-new member for $200 on day one.

Most clubs settle on quarterly. It sits in the middle on both counts and lines up with the rhythm of a season. **Dues can be called monthly and still be collected four times a year.**

## 📆 Handle joiners and leavers by the month

Don't pro-rate by the day. Start doing daily arithmetic every time somebody moves and the arithmetic costs more than the dues.

Deciding on whole months reduces every case to one question: were they a member that month? Someone joining mid-month pays from that month, someone leaving mid-month pays to that month. Round up or round down as you prefer; writing the rule down is the part that matters.

The one case worth settling in advance is a long absence for injury or study abroad. Waived, halved, or unchanged? Fixed costs like pitch hire don't fall when attendance does, so waiving them entirely pushes the cost onto everyone still playing. The logic is the one in [when someone joins a group halfway through](/en/blog/adding-members-midway/): where something is shared, membership itself generates cost, and saying so makes the rule easy to defend.

## 🔔 Let the record do the chasing

Chasing is unpleasant because the treasurer has to do it as a person. Naming a sum to a teammate is uncomfortable for anyone.

The way to lighten it is to change the message from "you haven't paid" to "here's where the record stands." When the record is somewhere everyone can see, the treasurer only has to keep it current.

[Share a project](/en/docs/projects/) in Evere, enter each payment and mark it settled, and what's outstanding shows up as a balance on screen. Point people at the record before messaging them individually. That alone removes most of the awkwardness, for the reasons set out in [why a settled record matters](/en/blog/settled-record-matters/).

## 🤝 Hand over the procedure, not the balance

Treasurers change almost every year, and what gets handed over is usually a balance and a bank card.

The balance isn't the useful part. What the next person needs is the procedure: the amount, the collection dates, who's exempt, how many are outstanding, and when the annual bills leave the account. One page with those five things on it and they can work from their first month.

Keeping club funds and event funds apart is the principle in [keeping the books for a club trip](/en/blog/club-camp-accounting/), and it pays off again at handover. **Books that have been mixed become unreadable the moment they change hands.**

## ❓ Common questions

**Q. Members are asking for lower dues.**
Show the breakdown of annual fixed costs before discussing the figure. Dues only come down when fixed costs do, which makes it a question about how the club operates rather than about the dues.

**Q. We want to stop handling cash.**
Moving to transfers makes the treasurer's bookkeeping far easier. If even one member can only pay in cash, make that single exception and absorb it in the record rather than forcing everyone onto one method.

**Q. We lose a whole cohort every summer.**
Before the year turns over, check whether next year's numbers still cover the fixed costs. Pitch hire doesn't shrink with the squad, and that window is the only sensible time to revise the rate.

**Q. Should we refund what's left over?**
If the dues were collected to be spent within the year, carrying the surplus into next year is normal. Say that you'll be carrying it over at the point you collect, not afterwards.
