Someone joins the trip on day two. A new housemate moves in. An extra pair of hands turns up midway through a project.

More people is good news everywhere except in the accounts, where it raises one awkward question: **how much of what's already been spent does the new person carry**.

## ➕ Think in expenses, not in dates

The first instinct is to work from the date they arrived. A five-day trip joined on day three, so three-fifths of everything.

That calculates cleanly and describes reality badly. Someone arriving on day three still uses the cooking oil bought on day one, and never slept in the room booked for the first two nights. **Apportion by days and what people used stops matching what they pay.**

The right unit is the expense, not the date. Take them one at a time and ask whether this person has anything to do with this expense. If yes, they're a participant; if no, they aren't. Evere lets you [choose the participants on each expense](/en/docs/expenses/), so that judgement goes straight into the record.

## 🧮 Almost nothing actually needs re-splitting

Once you think per expense, the job of "going back and redoing everything" turns out to barely exist.

Most finished expenses are already complete with the people who were there. Dinner on day two has nothing to do with someone who arrived on day three. An expense they have no part in simply doesn't need touching.

The exception is anything paid for earlier that you're all about to use together: shared things. The accommodation, the hire car, the bulk shop, the equipment bought for the group. Those get judged on the period of use, not the date of payment.

| Kind of expense | Does the late arrival go in? |
|---|---|
| A given day's meals | Only the days they were there |
| Individual travel | Only journeys they took |
| Accommodation, hire car | Apportion by nights or days used |
| Bulk food and equipment | Yes, because they'll use it |

## 🏠 Shared things still reach a late arrival

Shared items are where this gets contentious.

Four people take a place for four nights at $400, already paid, split $100 each. A fifth joins from day two, so three of the four nights now have five people in them.

Done strictly, night one splits four ways and the rest split five. But that only works if the accommodation can be divided by night at all. And there's a detail people miss: **if the number of rooms didn't change, neither did the total**. A fifth person who didn't increase the bill is a reason everyone pays less, not a reason to collect extra.

Ongoing arrangements like a houseshare behave differently, where pro-rating a mid-month move-in is standard. That logic is in [splitting rent in a houseshare](/en/blog/roomshare-rent-split/).

## 🔒 A finished collection stays closed

There's one exception. Once you've announced an amount and finished collecting it, a new arrival doesn't reopen it.

A group gift is the classic case. Ten people agreed on $30 each; an eleventh asks to join. You don't drop everyone to $27.27, because that means refunding people who already paid, and the effort exceeds what the extra person adds. That case is covered in [collecting for a group gift](/en/blog/group-gift-collection/).

The line runs like this: **anything already collected against a fixed amount stays put; anything not yet settled gets its participants reviewed per expense**. Keep those two apart and each new arrival stops being a decision.

## 🤝 Charging for the past makes joining expensive

Separately from getting the arithmetic right, there's something else worth weighing. Billing a late arrival for what came before makes joining itself feel heavy.

Invite someone with "come along whenever" and then ask them for costs dating from day one, and next time they'll hesitate before saying yes. Whether the figure was accurate matters much less, and for much less time, than how hard it felt to join.

For a group that continues, keep the cost of joining low and the cost of staying equal. Asking for a share of past spending makes sense only where there's an asset they can take a piece of when they leave.

## ❓ Common questions

**Q. How do we handle someone leaving partway instead?**
Same logic in reverse: drop them from the participants on expenses after they go. For shared items already paid for, decide upfront whether to apportion by use or write it off.

**Q. Recalculating every time the headcount changes is a chore.**
Pick participants per expense and there's nothing to recalculate. Managing a per-head figure in your head is what forces a full redo on every change.

**Q. The new person wants to pay towards earlier expenses.**
Accept if they've offered. But base the figure on the expenses that genuinely involve them rather than on days. Letting someone overpay out of goodwill removes the benchmark for whoever leaves next.

**Q. We bought extra equipment because of the new person.**
Record that purchase as everyone's, including them. Unless it's exclusively theirs, there's no reason it lands on the newest arrival alone.
