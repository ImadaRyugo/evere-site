At the cabin, the host asks for a $300 damage deposit. At a hotel abroad, the front desk holds an amount on your card on top of the room rate. Renting skis or a camera often comes with a deposit too.

These deposits are awkward money in a trip budget. They look like a payment, and then they come back. **Put money you expect to get back into the split, and you end up redoing the math the day it is returned.**

## 🛡️ A deposit is money held, not money spent

Start with what a deposit actually is. The room or the rental fee is gone the moment you pay. A deposit is not. If nothing goes wrong, the full amount returns to whoever put it down.

So the default is to leave it out of the split when it is paid. The only part that ever needs splitting is whatever does not come back: the deduction.

Treating it this way removes a record. Instead of logging it once when paid and again when refunded, you log one item, and only if something was deducted.

## 💳 A card authorization never goes in the split

Hotels in many countries place a temporary hold on your credit card at check-in, as a deposit against the minibar or damage. If nothing happens, the hold is released after checkout.

That hold is not a charge. It may show up on your card for a while, but **record it as an advance and you are splitting money that nobody actually paid.**

Record only what is actually charged at checkout. If someone used the minibar, enter that amount as theirs. Holds can take days to drop off, and there is no need to rush the settle-up while it is still visible.

## 🧮 Recording a cash deposit

Four friends rent a cabin. A paid the whole rental when booking. At check-in, B paid the $300 damage deposit. At checkout, $60 was deducted for a glass C broke, and B got $240 back.

| Money movement | Amount | Record it? | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rental (A, at booking) | $1,200 | Yes | Paid by A, split equally among four |
| Deposit (B, at check-in) | $300 | No | Expected to come back |
| Deduction (broken glass) | $60 | Yes | Paid by B, owed by C |
| Refund (back to B) | $240 | No | Just the deposit returning |

Two records in all. To settle, B, C and D each pay A $300, and C pays B $60. Had you split the $300 deposit four ways at check-in, you would have to hand the $240 back out in reverse when it was returned.

Folding refunds and deductions into a split after the fact is covered in [a refund lands after you split it](/en/blog/refund-resettlement/), but a deposit is money you knew from the start would come back, so the simplest move is never to put it in.

## 🧯 Why it was deducted decides who pays

When something is deducted, the reason decides whose cost it is.

If one person's action caused it, it is theirs. A broken glass, damaged gear, a late return. The person usually knows, and it rarely turns into an argument.

If everyone shares the cause, split it among everyone. Trash not sorted, the place left messier than expected, a noise complaint. These are hard to pin on anyone, and **agreeing that anything without a clear owner gets split among all of you keeps the mood intact.** In the example above, if nobody knew who broke the glass, the $60 would be $15 each.

In Evere, when you record the deduction, just choose as participants the people who owe it: only C, or all four.

## ❓ Common questions

**Q. What if the deposit is refunded by transfer days later?**
It goes back to whoever paid it, so you can settle the rest of the trip first. Once the deduction is final, add that single item and settle it. It works the same as [something turns up after you've settled](/en/blog/post-settlement-additions/).

**Q. We paid the deposit in a foreign currency and got slightly less back after conversion.**
Treat the difference as part of the trip's cost and split it among everyone. How to think about exchange rates is in [which exchange rate to use when splitting](/en/blog/exchange-rate-for-splitting/).

**Q. The deposit always ends up on whoever has the highest card limit.**
If it is only a hold, there is no real cost to them, so whoever can, should. Charges like the minibar can land on that card later, though, so have everyone look over the checkout bill.

**Q. The person who paid a cash deposit has to leave early. Now what?**
Switch who collects the refund to someone staying. Until that person hands it over, leave the advance records as they are.
