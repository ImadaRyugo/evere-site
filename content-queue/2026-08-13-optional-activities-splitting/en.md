Day two of a trip for five. Three of you are going diving, the other two are walking around town, and everyone is having dinner together in the evening. Working out how to split that day is where the mood tightens slightly.

It seems obvious that people who don't go shouldn't pay for the activity. In practice, though, **the harder part is saying so out loud**. "I'm not going, so I'm not paying for it" is a reasonable sentence that almost nobody wants to be the one to say.

## 🎟️ Assume the group will split up, and say so early

Agree this before the trip and the awkwardness essentially disappears. There's only one thing to settle: costs that only some people take part in are paid only by those people.

Saying it in advance means nobody becomes the villain for opting out on the day. Choosing not to go stops being a small betrayal, which also means fewer people joining things they have no interest in out of obligation. **The fastest way to ruin a group trip is to require everyone to do everything.**

The principle is all you need. Which specific costs count as exceptions can be judged in the moment.

## 🧾 Default: only the participants pay

Tickets, activity fees, guides, equipment hire. Whatever only some people received, only those people split. There's nothing to debate here.

One person usually pays for the whole booking, so the work happens when you record it. Enter one expense for three people's diving and **restrict who it applies to**. Skip that step and it quietly gets spread across all five.

The bigger the activity, the worse this omission gets. You can fix it later, of course, but numbers that move right before settlement are the hardest ones to explain.

## 🍽️ The exceptions are the shared containers

Even on a day when the group splits, some things stay whole. Accommodation, the rental car, parking, that morning's breakfast. Everyone is inside those regardless of where they went afterwards.

The test is whether the person who skipped still benefited. Someone who didn't dive but got a lift into town in the hire car benefited from the car. Conversely, if the divers took a boat that nobody else used, that boat belongs to the three of them.

Transport blurs the line quickly, but **when in doubt, splitting it across everyone causes less lasting friction** than being precise. Arguing over a few dollars costs more than it saves; carve out only the obviously large items.

## 💸 For anything booked in advance, share the cancellation deadline

Activities you can decide on the morning of cause no problems. The awkward ones are those you book from home with a fixed headcount.

If someone who said yes doesn't show up, you're left with either a cancellation fee or a wasted place. Who absorbs that should be settled when you book, not when it happens. Share the free-cancellation deadline with everyone and add one line: after this date, a no-show pays for their own place.

It's worth touching on illness and injury at the same moment. In the middle of it, whoever states the rule ends up looking heartless, no matter how reasonable the rule is.

## 🧮 A day with a half-day split

Alex books $540 of diving for three. Dana pays $24 in museum entry for the two in town. Bo covers the $250 dinner, and Chris pays $120 for that day's car.

| | What they're in on | Owes |
|---|---|---|
| Alex | diving + dinner + car | $254 |
| Bo | diving + dinner + car | $254 |
| Chris | diving + dinner + car | $254 |
| Dana | museum + dinner + car | $86 |
| Eli | museum + dinner + car | $86 |

Dinner is 250 ÷ 5 = $50 and the car is 120 ÷ 5 = $24, both across everyone. Diving is $180 each for three people only; museum entry is $12 each for two. The column totals $934, which matches what was actually spent that day.

Subtract what each person fronted and Alex receives $286, while Bo pays $4, Chris $134, Dana $62 and Eli $86. Both sides come to $286.

## ❓ Common questions

**Someone says "don't worry about my share." Then what?**
Accept it in the moment, but record the real amount anyway. People who wave off their share usually overpay somewhere else on the same trip, and a record makes that visible. Write nothing down and the generous person simply ends up down on the deal.

**Everyone was going, then one person pulls out on the day.**
If the booking is non-refundable, that place is theirs to pay for. Genuinely unavoidable reasons like illness are a fair case for splitting it, though. Deciding which way you lean in advance keeps the call from turning into a judgement about that person.

**The group that stayed behind spent much less. Do we even it out?**
No. Spending less was the consequence of a choice, not a windfall. Trying to level it pushes the group straight back to doing everything together, which defeats the point of allowing the split in the first place.

**What about someone who only got a lift?**
Fuel, tolls and parking get split among whoever was in the car. For the labour of driving, the reasoning in [splitting rental car costs](/en/blog/rental-car-trip-costs/) applies here too.
