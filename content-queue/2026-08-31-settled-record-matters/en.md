The part of splitting a bill that outlasts everything else isn't who owes what. It's that **when two people disagree about whether the money was handed over, there's no way to check**.

Any calculator can do the division. The hard problem starts afterwards, with "I sent that ages ago" against "I never got it", and neither person lying. This piece is about the record that stops that scene from happening at all.

## Both memories are telling the truth

Treating the disagreement as somebody forgetting leads to the wrong fix. What's actually happening is that the intention to send and the absence of a receipt both exist, correctly, inside their respective heads.

Someone who opened the transfer screen, got interrupted by a notification and closed it will remember the whole sequence as having paid. Someone who receives small amounts constantly cannot tell which incoming transfer belonged to which dinner. Neither is a failure of memory. It's that memory doesn't store amounts attached to events in the first place.

So the countermeasure isn't to remember harder. It's to build something that doesn't require remembering.

## Three states let you describe what actually happens

If a settlement can only be done or not done, real life doesn't fit. Handing over $50 of an $80 debt is neither.

| State | Meaning | Later calculations |
|---|---|---|
| Unsettled | Nothing handed over yet | Includes the full amount |
| Partly settled | Some of it handed over | Includes only the remainder |
| Settled | Handed over in full | Excluded |

Evere's [settlement screen](/en/docs/settlement/) carries all three. Receive $50 against an $80 balance and the remaining $30 carries into the next calculation as a partial settlement. Mark it settled once the rest arrives and it drops out of the maths entirely.

The practical value of a middle state shows up **wherever cash and app transfers are mixed together**. "Here's $20 in cash now, I'll send the rest" stops being a thing the record has no room for.

## Design it so it can be undone

A record that can't be corrected stops being used. If a button is frightening to press, nobody presses it.

In Evere, anything marked as settled can be **put back with a single undo**. That one property is what makes "just log it and check later" a workable habit. Accuracy in a record comes from being able to fix mistakes, not from the effort of never making them.

There are three levels of granularity too: an individual expense, a single who-pays-whom line on the settlement screen, or a whole project at once. A trip with a clear ending is fastest settled in one action at the end. Ongoing household costs suit clearing them line by line, because there's no end to reach.

## How much detail depends on how long the relationship runs

Nothing here requires recording everything precisely. The question isn't the size of the amounts, it's how much longer these people will be settling things with each other.

For a one-off gathering, settle on the day and close the record with it. In a relationship that repeats every month, the record turns into something worth having. What the electricity actually cost six months ago, or who was consistently out of pocket on last year's trip, cannot be reconstructed from memory.

The other factor is headcount. Two people can track it in conversation. Past four, the number of possible who-paid-whom pairs grows beyond what anyone holds in their head. For cutting the number of transfers themselves, see [how to minimise settlements](/en/blog/minimize-settlements/); for where spreadsheets and chat apps stop coping as a record, [comparing the tools people use](/en/blog/warikan-tools-compared/) covers it.

## A record is not an invoice

Some people resist marking things settled because it looks like distrust, as though a friend's word wasn't enough.

It's worth separating the two ideas. A record isn't an instrument for doubting the other person, it's **insurance against your own forgetting damaging the friendship**. Precisely because you trust them, you arrange things so nobody's memory has to be the authority. In practice, groups that keep settlement records end up discussing money less often, not more, because having a way to check removes the need to check.

If you do share it, share it as a report rather than a demand. Evere can share the settlement result as formatted text, so what arrives is a summary. With the figures on screen first, the conversation shifts from "pay me back" to "does this look right to you".

## Common questions

**Q. Is there any point recording cash handovers?**
Cash is the case that needs it most. App transfers leave a history on their own; cash leaves nothing. If the person handing it over logs it, the fact survives even when the person receiving it forgets.

**Q. What if I spot a wrong amount after marking it settled?**
Undo the settlement first, then fix the number. Editing the amount while it stays marked settled leaves the record disagreeing with what was actually handed over. The undo exists so that stepping back is always the safe move.

**Q. Does everyone in the group need the same app for this to work?**
One person recording is enough. Requiring everyone to enter their own expenses guarantees somebody won't, so it's more realistic to give the job to one person from the start. Sharing the result gets the numbers to people who never installed anything.

**Q. When exactly should something be marked settled?**
Right after the money actually moves. Marking it before you send means a forgotten transfer sits in the record as complete. Fix the order as hand it over, then log it.
