A ballot for sought-after tickets, a limited release, a new year lucky bag. People enter as a group to improve the odds, or to share out what's inside.

What's left over isn't a money problem. **Money divides easily; the thing that won does not.**

## 🍀 Decide before the draw, because afterwards you can't

The conclusion first: agree the method before you enter. Discussing it after the result is announced always goes badly.

The reason is simple. The moment a result lands, everyone's position changes. The winner has something in hand they'd rather keep, and everyone else is left holding the fact that they entered too. **The same person genuinely wants different things before and after a draw**, which is why the decision has to come first.

Three options cover almost everything.

| Method | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Shared by all | The win belongs to the group; whoever uses it pays the others | High-value items |
| Entrant's own | The win belongs to whoever entered, and so does the cost | Everyone entering separately |
| Agreed order | Wins are taken in a pre-agreed order | When several may land |

## 🎫 With tickets, settle who goes before you enter

Four people enter a ballot that awards two tickets. One person wins; two can attend.

Agree in advance that "whoever wins goes" and the only remaining question is who gets the second seat. Without that, the winner ends up choosing a companion, and whoever isn't chosen has nothing they can reasonably say.

The practical version is a running order set before entering: if anyone wins, seats fill in this sequence. Whoever takes a seat pays the winner for the ticket. That's the whole rule. Where travel is involved, [settling up on a gig trip](/en/blog/fan-trip-group-expenses/) covers the rest of the costs.

## 🎁 A lucky bag assumes the contents are unequal

Lucky bags and prize draws behave differently from tickets. Everybody gets something, and **you know from the outset that what each person gets is worth a different amount.**

Declaring that luck is luck is a perfectly good rule. If everyone genuinely accepts it, it is also the least work.

It gets harder to accept as the gap widens. Three people buy a $120 bag between them, and the contents come to $80, $50 and $20 at retail. Everyone paid $40 and one person is holding four times what another is.

## 🧮 Value the contents and move only the difference

In that case, take the retail values as a ratio and re-split the cost along it.

Retail comes to $150 and the bag cost $120, so everyone effectively bought at the same discount.

| | Retail value | Cost by ratio | vs paying equally |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | $80 | $64 | +$24 |
| B | $50 | $40 | $0 |
| C | $20 | $16 | −$24 |
| Total | $150 | $120 | $0 |

A hands over $24, C receives $24, done. **Don't unwind the $40 everyone already paid — move the difference only.** To record it, [split by exact amounts](/en/docs/expenses/) and enter the figures above.

Don't force a value onto anything without a clear retail price. Convert what can be converted, treat the rest as luck, and the conversation ends sooner.

## 💬 Passing it on, selling it, cashing it in

Draw the line on reselling in advance.

If one person sells something the group entered for, whose is the money? The same logic as [selling something you bought together](/en/blog/selling-shared-items/) applies: dividing by the contribution ratio is the version you can explain.

Whether to permit reselling at all is a separate decision. Ban it and anyone who receives something they don't want has no exit. Providing one — the group buys it off them, or they get priority in the next entry — is what makes the rule survive contact with reality.

## ❓ Common questions

**Q. How do we split entry fees?**
They're incurred whether you win or lose, so divide them equally among everyone who entered. Load them onto the winner and nobody will enter next time.

**Q. The same person keeps winning.**
Trying to correct the distribution of luck with money empties the group out. Look only at the value of each round's contents, never at how often someone has won.

**Q. What if it's trading above retail?**
Market prices move, so the answer changes depending on when you look, and agreement gets harder. Fixing on retail avoids the argument later.

**Q. Nobody wants what's left.**
Sell it as a lot and divide the proceeds by the ratio. Pushing it onto someone shrinks the group next time.
