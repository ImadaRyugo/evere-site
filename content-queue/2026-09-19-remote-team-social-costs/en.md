Organise an online social and the first thing that stalls isn't the date. It's the money.

In person you divide the bill by the number of people. Online there is no bill to divide. Everyone is eating and drinking at home at their own expense, and you still have to produce a shared number from that.

## 🖥️ An online social has no basis for a contribution

An in-person contribution is spending that already happened, divided by heads. The basis came first and the figure followed.

Online the order flips. **You set the amount first and work out what it's for afterwards.** That reversal is what makes it hard for people to accept. Paying upfront for something unspecified is a structure that never came up in person.

So the first job isn't picking a number. It's making the purpose concrete. Delivery to each person's door, prizes for a quiz, an outside host. **Once the purpose is fixed the amount produces itself.** Set a contribution while the purpose is still vague and the questions start arriving the moment you send the message.

## 📦 Sending physical things means the amounts won't match

A popular format is delivering the same thing to everyone's home. It creates a shared moment, and people rate it highly.

The money doesn't divide cleanly, though, because shipping depends on the address. The same $30 box costs $5 to deliver locally and $15 to somewhere remote. Anything chilled or frozen widens the gap further.

| Item | City address | Remote address |
|---|---|---|
| The box | $30 | $30 |
| Shipping | $5 | $15 |
| Total | $35 | $45 |

Billing the true cost means paying more for living further away. Pooling shipping across everyone and folding it into the price causes the least friction. Quote the total divided by heads as the contribution, and be ready to show the breakdown if asked.

## 🌏 The same figure weighs differently by location

A remote team assumes people are spread out. Across borders, an identical amount stops meaning the same thing.

Ask for the same contribution from someone in a very different cost-of-living country and it can be several times the real burden. Scale the amount instead and you end up discussing who belongs in which bracket, in front of everybody.

The workable landing point is to **keep the contribution flat, and make sure money never decides whether someone attends**. If the price is what makes a person decline, that defeats the point of the event. Either the company covers part of it, or attendance is optional with a capped ask. Either one gives people a way out.

## 💳 No cash means collecting in advance

In-person events always had a fallback: collect cash on the night. Online has no such thing.

Collection therefore happens by transfer, upfront. Which creates the problem of not knowing who has paid until the day. The organiser ends up checking people off one at a time, and that work scales with the headcount.

The fix is keeping who-paid-what in one place. [Set up a project for the social](/en/docs/projects/) in Evere and the payments show up as a list. You message only the people still outstanding, instead of sending another all-hands reminder.

## 🧾 Draw the line between company and personal early

Remote socials blur the line between work and a private gathering, and that blur is what makes the money conversation hard.

The test is whether attendance is optional. Everyone, during work hours, means the company should pay. Volunteers, outside work hours, means people pay their own way. Avoid creating a middle case, because the middle is what can't be explained.

If the company covers part of it, say which part before anything is bought. "Food and drink on the company, prizes on us" settles every later question. It's the same exercise as separating company expenses from personal splits in [business trip costs](/en/blog/business-trip-advance/).

## ❓ Common questions

**Q. We don't charge non-attendees, but should they still get the box?**
Keep it to attendees. Someone receiving the goods without paying makes it awkward for them to decline next time. Contribution and delivery should line up.

**Q. Time zones mean some people join late or leave early.**
Don't discount them. Pro-rating by minutes means deciding where the cut-off sits. Choosing a time that works for them in the first place addresses the real issue.

**Q. Do we refund a no-show?**
Not where physical items were sent — the money is already spent. The same applies to prizes or streaming costs, anything that can't be unbought. Say so in advance and nobody objects.

**Q. Who absorbs the transfer fee for overseas members?**
Let the organiser carry it inside the contribution. If the recipient absorbs it, they're effectively contributing more, and money arriving short with no explanation is the hardest version to talk about.
