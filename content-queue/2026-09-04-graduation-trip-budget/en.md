Graduation trips stall not because the group can't pick a destination, but because **everyone has a different budget and nobody will say so out loud**.

Part-time hours differ, family support differs, the job waiting after graduation differs. Since "I don't want to come" is nearly impossible to say, anyone who can't afford it invents a different reason and quietly drops out. The per-person cost then rises, and the next person drops out. That's the order it collapses in.

## 🚌 Set the ceiling before the destination

Reverse the order. Before anyone suggests places, collect what each person can spend.

The way you ask matters. Asking for a figure invites people to inflate it, so offer bands instead: under $500, under $800, or $1,000 and up, answered anonymously. Set the ceiling at the lowest band that comes back and nobody has to bow out.

Choosing a destination after the ceiling exists is also faster. A fixed budget looks like a constraint, but **narrowing the options is exactly what makes the decision quick**, so the whole planning phase shrinks.

## 🧮 Separate fixed costs from discretionary ones

Quote one total figure and nobody can decide whether they're coming. Split the number in two: what's locked in the moment you join, and what moves with each person's choices.

Here's a three-night trip for four people.

| Item | Total | How it splits | Per person |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accommodation (one room, three nights) | $960 | Evenly | $240 |
| Travel there and back | Booked individually | Each | $300 |
| Car hire and fuel | $240 | Evenly | $60 |
| Food and activities | Individual | Each | $200 |

Accommodation is 960 ÷ 4 = $240 and the car is 240 ÷ 4 = $60. Added up, 240 + 300 + 60 + 200 gives $800.

Of that, only the $300 for accommodation and the car is genuinely unavoidable. The remaining $500 moves with how each person travels. Showing the split this way lets somebody on a tight budget work out for themselves that eating cheaply makes it possible. **What people decide against isn't the total, it's the committed portion.**

## 💳 Most of it is paid upfront, so decide who fronts what

On a graduation trip, accommodation and transport are paid weeks before departure. Put the whole lot on one person's card and they're lending the group several hundred before the trip even starts.

There are two ways around it. Spread the payments by category, so accommodation goes on one card, the hire car on another, tolls on a third, and no single person is out of pocket by much. Or collect money once, at the point the bookings are confirmed. Settling everything after you get home is a poor fit for a trip this size.

## 🙅 Build the trip so nobody has to opt out

Even with a ceiling, the gap can show up on the ground: an entry fee everyone pays, an add-on activity, drinks after dinner.

The fix is to build in the option not to join. Put one block of free time in the itinerary, and skipping something becomes ordinary rather than a statement. Plan every hour as a group activity and stepping out turns into an announcement about your finances. For the activities themselves, [what to do about the ones people skip](/en/blog/optional-activities-splitting/) covers the arithmetic.

Don't let anyone quietly top up someone else's share. Even when it's kind, the person covered finds it harder to speak up the next time. If a gap is going to be closed, close it in a way everyone knows about, or plan a trip where the gap doesn't arise.

## 🗂️ Don't leave one person holding all of it

Graduation trips become gruelling when booking, collecting and scheduling all sit with the same person. Split it three ways.

The booker handles accommodation and transport and pays for them. The treasurer tracks who paid what and runs the final settlement. The planner builds the timetable and the local logistics. These three jobs don't peak at the same time, so dividing them adds almost no coordination overhead.

Having a named treasurer also gives money questions a single destination. Simply removing the state where everybody asks everybody "did you pay for that?" lifts the mood of the group noticeably. The wider running order is in [managing money on a group trip](/en/blog/group-trip-money-guide/).

## ❓ Common questions

**Q. How do I collect budgets anonymously?**
A form works, or just have people message the organiser privately. What matters is that nobody learns who picked which band. Publish it and the person on the lowest number is identified, which puts them right back where they started.

**Q. What if someone pulls out partway through planning?**
It depends on whether the booking can be reduced. If it can, refund the difference and you're done. If it can't, their share still exists. Share the date cancellation charges start with everyone before you book anything.

**Q. Some of us have student discounts and some don't.**
Let the discount reduce the bill of the person who holds it. Splitting the saving across everyone means the discount holder gains nothing, and from then on nobody mentions having one.

**Q. Nobody carries cash. What then?**
One person paying and logging it is still the fastest arrangement. Just rotate who that is: person A on day one, person B on day two, so the fronted amounts don't pile onto one card.
