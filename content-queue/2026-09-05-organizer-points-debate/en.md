Paying cash never produced this question. Now that one person puts everyone's dinner on a single card, there's a new one: what do we think about that person collecting the cashback or the air miles on their own?

Nothing improper is happening. They front the money and recover exactly the same amount. Yet plenty of people feel a vague sense that it isn't quite right, and the feeling tends to stay unspoken. It's worth taking apart.

## The instinct has something real behind it

The discomfort comes from where the benefit originates. Everyone contributed equally, and the thing that grew out of those contributions goes to one person. **Shared payment, private return** is a structure that doesn't sit comfortably beside the premise of splitting a bill.

Dismissing that instinct as petty misses the point, because the identical structure at a larger scale bothers everyone. If ten people spent five thousand on a holiday, one person put it all on a card, and quietly kept the rewards, that would get discussed. The only reason a dinner doesn't provoke the same reaction is the size of the number.

## The number is smaller than it feels

So how much is it? Ten people at $50 each puts $500 through the card. At a one per cent rate, that's $5.

Now count the organiser's work. Finding somewhere, booking it, herding the replies, collecting on the night, settling up the next day. Two hours if it goes smoothly. Five dollars over two hours is $2.50 an hour.

Which means it sits in the most awkward band possible: **too small to be a fee, too visible to ignore**. The organiser isn't volunteering for the rewards, and the group doesn't seriously want them handed back. Nobody's heart is in it, and yet the argument survives.

## What bothers people isn't the money

Two hours of low-grade irritation over five dollars only makes sense once you see that the money isn't the subject. The problem is that it happened **without anyone saying so**.

The same $5, announced in advance as "I'll stick it on my card, so I'm keeping the points," bothers nobody. It usually gets a thank-you for taking the job on. Discovered afterwards, the amount stops mattering and the feeling that arrives is simply that you weren't told.

This is the same shape as other splitting disputes. Being charged for something small stings not because of the sum but because the premise was never shared. What [chasing small amounts](/en/blog/small-amounts-cheap/) covers applies here in reverse.

## Decide it and there's nothing left to argue about

There are three workable arrangements. Any of them is fine. What matters is that you say which one you're using.

The first is stating plainly that the organiser keeps them. Most groups land here, because it reads as payment for the work and everyone can follow the logic. The second is deducting the rewards from what you collect, so a $500 dinner is charged as $495. It adds a calculation and removes any need to justify anything. The third is rotating who pays on the larger occasions, which evens out on its own in a group that meets a few times a year.

On expensive trips, more people pick the second or third. Once the bill passes a thousand, the rewards pass ten, and **it stops being an amount that needs no explanation**. Where the line sits is for each group to decide.

## Losing your organisers costs far more

One thing gets overlooked in this argument. Push it too hard and nobody volunteers to organise anything.

Organisers carry the booking, the money they front, and the chasing afterwards, financially and mentally. A group that insists on settling their five dollars precisely will find nobody stepping forward for the next dinner. **Tilting things slightly towards whoever did the work isn't a failure of fairness, it's the cost of the group continuing to exist.** On what fairness even means here, [three principles of a fair split](/en/blog/what-fair-splitting-means/) sets them out.

There's only one thing you actually have to settle: who pays, and what ends up with whom afterwards. Say it out loud, and [keep the amounts recorded](/en/blog/drinking-party-organizer-guide/). Do that and the points stop being a controversy and turn into an arrangement.
