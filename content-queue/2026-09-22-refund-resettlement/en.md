The accommodation got cancelled. The restaurant refunded the deposit. The tickets were reimbursed.

The money moving is simple enough. The awkward part is that **the amount changed after the split was already done**. Until you decide how far to unwind, nothing moves.

## ↩️ A refund is a new record, not an undo

The first decision is whether to edit the original expense or add the refund as its own entry.

Rewriting the amount looks tidy. If a $200 booking becomes $150, change it to $150 and the arithmetic works out.

It has one flaw. **What you paid and what came back both disappear from the record.** Six months later there's no way to tell whether it was $150 from the start, or $200 with $50 returned. Ask what the cancellation fee was and nobody can answer.

Logging the refund as a negative expense matches what actually happened. The original stays, the refund sits on its own line, the history reads properly and the fee remains visible.

## 🔓 The steps change once it's marked settled

How hard a refund is to handle depends entirely on whether the settlement is already closed.

If it isn't, this is easy. Record the refund and it feeds into the next calculation on its own. Nobody has handed over anything yet, so only the difference needs adjusting.

If the settlement is already done, there's one extra step. Evere lets you [unsettle it to revert](/en/docs/settlement/), so the correct order is to unsettle it, record the refund, then settle again. Moving amounts while it stays marked settled leaves the record disagreeing with what actually changed hands.

The principle of recording on the assumption you can reverse it runs through [why marking a debt settled matters](/en/blog/settled-record-matters/).

## 💳 The money goes back to whoever fronted it

The overlooked detail is who receives the refund. It returns down the path it came, which means **the whole amount lands with the one person who paid**.

Say four of you booked a $200 place and one person put it on their card. When $150 comes back, it goes into that person's account. To the other three, how much of their $50 is coming back is invisible.

So when a refund arrives, share the fact that you received it before you work out the numbers. Say nothing and the structure looks exactly like the person who fronted it quietly profiting. No bad intent required — the appearance alone is enough to cause an argument.

## ✂️ A partial refund is a different problem

A refund with a cancellation fee deducted is harder than a full one, because it adds a question: who absorbs the part that didn't come back.

| Situation | Returned | How to divide |
|---|---|---|
| Full refund | $200 | Nothing to divide |
| Partial, one person's own cancellation | $150 | The $50 fee is theirs |
| Partial, the group called it off | $150 | The $50 fee splits by head |

The test is whether the cancellation came from one person or from the group. Personal reasons, personal fee. Weather or a collective decision, split it. Agreeing that line in advance makes the conversation short when a cancellation actually happens.

Who carries the fee is set out in full in [who pays the cancellation fee](/en/blog/cancellation-fee-who-pays/).

## ⚖️ Deciding not to redo it is also an answer

Not every refund needs a re-split. Sometimes the work of recalculating exceeds the money in motion.

Judge it on the per-person change. If splitting four ways moves each person by three dollars, carrying it to the next occasion is the realistic call. If it moves each of them by fifty, redo it now.

What matters is saying that you've decided not to. **Quietly not processing it and agreeing together not to process it produce completely different outcomes.** The first leaves a residue and the second doesn't. It's the deciding, not the amount, that people remember.

## ❓ Common questions

**Q. The refund came back as points or a voucher.**
No cash returned, so leaving the split alone is simpler. Whoever holds it uses it next time, and you account for it against that expense instead.

**Q. The refund takes weeks to arrive.**
Record it once it's confirmed and settle without waiting. If the refund is certain, the date it lands doesn't change anything.

**Q. The refund only concerns some of the group.**
Record it with only those people as participants. An expense split among everyone and a refund affecting a few work correctly as long as they're separate records.

**Q. Cash had already changed hands when the refund appeared.**
Don't delete the handover. Treat the refund as a new balance between you. Cancelling the record of money that genuinely moved makes the sequence impossible to follow later.
