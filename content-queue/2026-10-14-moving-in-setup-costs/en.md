Moving in together costs more than people expect. Deposit, agency fees, first month's rent, the move itself, furniture and appliances. Several thousand, routinely.

And most couples handle all of it with "half each." **An even split looks fair, and the things inside a setup cost behave completely differently from one another.**

## 🛋️ Setup costs come in four kinds

It all leaves the same account, which makes it tempting to treat as one number. It isn't one number.

| Kind | Examples | What happens to it |
|---|---|---|
| Money that may return | Deposit, bond | Refunded to the named tenant |
| Money that's gone | Agency fees, first rent, admin | Never comes back |
| Cost of moving | Removals, packing materials | Never comes back |
| Things that remain | Furniture, appliances, curtains | Still exist as objects |

Split all four evenly and **the money that returns and the things that remain follow the name on the paperwork, not the person who paid.** That's the part that surfaces later.

## 🧮 Sorting a real total into four

Take $6,000 of setup costs.

| Item | Amount | Half each |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit | $1,200 | $600 |
| Agency fees, first rent, admin | $1,800 | $900 |
| Removals | $800 | $400 |
| Furniture and appliances | $2,200 | $1,100 |
| Total | $6,000 | $3,000 |

Split evenly, that's $3,000 each. The problem arrives at the end of the tenancy, when the $1,200 deposit is refunded to whoever signed. If the other person contributed $600 of it, that $600 was **fronting money destined for someone else's account.**

So agree one sentence when you sign: the deposit, when refunded, is divided in the proportions it was paid. Without it, money returning years later simply belongs to the named tenant. The settling-up itself is covered in [what to sort out when you stop living together](/en/blog/cohabitation-breakup-settlement/), but the work that prevents the argument happens on move-in day.

## 🪑 Decide who owns the furniture as you buy it

The $2,200 of furniture works the same way. A sofa bought half each belongs to both of you, and cannot be cut in half when you stop sharing an address.

Pick one of three as you buy: jointly owned, owned by one of you, or each buying their own.

The practical division is large things jointly, personal things individually. Fridge and washing machine joint; desk and chair belong to whoever uses them. For the joint items, agreeing to divide along the contribution ratio — as in [selling something you bought together](/en/blog/selling-shared-items/) — turns disposal into arithmetic rather than negotiation.

## ⚖️ Where incomes differ, setup costs especially should be proportional

Plenty of couples who split monthly costs by income still split setup costs down the middle.

Because the sum is large, an even split here lands hard. If incomes run 2:1, setup costs at 2:1 is the consistent answer. Setting the ratio works the same way as in [proportional splitting by income](/en/blog/income-based-split-couples/), and once set it carries over to the monthly numbers.

Family help adds another branch. If one set of parents paid for the furniture, is it jointly owned, or an asset belonging to their side? Start living together without answering that and it turns up inside an unrelated argument later, exactly as described in [when one set of parents helps out](/en/blog/parental-support-couple-budget/).

## 📋 Five things to settle before moving day

None of them are amounts. They're all about how amounts get treated.

Whose name is on the contract. How a refunded deposit gets divided. Which furniture is joint. Whether rent and living costs are even or proportional. And where this record lives.

The last one is the one people skip. [Create a project](/en/docs/projects/) in Evere holding the setup costs alone. Keep it separate from the monthly budget and years later you can still read the setup costs on their own. The monthly side is covered in [splitting expenses when you live together](/en/blog/cohabitation-expense-split/).

## ❓ Common questions

**Q. One of us brought furniture from home.**
It belongs to them. If you want to credit them for reducing the joint spend, reduce their share of the other setup costs a little. The figure doesn't need a market basis, only your agreement.

**Q. The named tenant pays the whole rent.**
The name and the burden can be managed separately. If one person pays and the other transfers half each month, a record makes that a genuine half.

**Q. I moved into a flat they already lived in.**
Most of the setup cost is already spent, so what's left to split is the move and any new furniture. Whether you retrospectively take half the deposit should be decided together with how a refund would be divided.

**Q. Keeping records is a hassle.**
Setup costs happen once. You're recording for a few days, not forever — it's less work than the monthly budget.
