Expat communities have one condition other groups don't: **a fixed share of the people leave every year**.

Someone arrives, takes on organising, and rotates home two years later. By the time the next person picks it up, nobody who knew how it used to work is still there. That's why the accounting gets rebuilt from scratch every couple of years.

## Tie it to the role, not the person

Handovers fail because the arrangements live in someone's memory. "Last year Marta paid up front and collected afterwards" is information that leaves when Marta does.

What you need written down isn't who does it but what happens in what order. What the ticket price is, when it's collected by, and what happens to anything left over. With those three in writing, the routine survives a change of organiser.

Write them somewhere everyone can see. Notes in the organiser's phone are not a handover document. Treat **anything stored on a personal device as leaving with that person**.

## If you carry a float, publish the balance every time

Small surpluses after an event are normal. The trouble starts when they roll forward.

| Event | Collected | Spent | Difference | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| April picnic | €240 | €215 | +€25 | €25 |
| July barbecue | €360 | €380 | -€20 | €5 |
| October sports day | €300 | €280 | +€20 | €25 |

April leaves €25, July covers a €20 shortfall from that balance and drops to €5, and October adds €20 to reach €25 again. The sums are trivial, but that €25 is always sitting in one specific person's wallet or on their card.

When that person moves away, the €25 has nowhere to go. So if you carry a float, **publish the balance and who is holding it after every event**. Naming the holder matters more than the number. Not carrying one is also a legitimate choice: zero it out each time and spend the remainder on drinks on the day, and there's one less thing to hand over.

## Keep everything in the local currency

In an expat group, everyone's mental currency is different. People who arrived last month still think in the currency they came from, long-timers think in the local one, and visitors from head office are mixed in with both.

Run the accounting in local currency only. Quote a ticket price in a home currency and each person converts at their own rate on their own day, so what arrives varies by person. Handle anyone who genuinely needs to pay in another currency as a named exception.

The only place conversion is unavoidable is settling with someone who's leaving. Even there, fixing the amount at the rate on the day the payment happened keeps a market move from becoming an argument. For handling two currencies at once, see [settling up on a trip home](/en/blog/home-visit-settlement/).

## Close each leaver out a month before they go

The biggest losses in community accounting happen at departure. Packing, farewells and paperwork all land in the same weeks, and the one thing that slips is the settling.

The moment someone's departure is confirmed, list everything attached to them: what they fronted, what they're holding, what they're still owed. Settle those three a month out and nobody has to talk about money in the final week.

The same applies to shared kit. Tents, cool boxes, speakers and the like disappear along with whoever had them at home unless the location is recorded. Keeping what it cost and who holds it means the next committee doesn't buy a second one.

## Put the record in the group, not in a place

The quickest way to make handovers light is to keep the amounts somewhere shared from the beginning. Once everyone sees the same screen regardless of who's organising, handing over becomes a matter of transferring access.

Evere lets you [keep a separate project per event](/en/docs/projects/) and invite the participants. With the picnic and the sports day in separate projects, the next organiser can look up exactly what last year cost. For running the event itself, [the organiser's guide to settling up](/en/blog/drinking-party-organizer-guide/) scales to larger gatherings too.

## Common questions

**Q. Attendance is unpredictable. How do we price tickets?**
Divide the fixed costs by fewer people than you expect. Venue hire and equipment don't shrink when people don't turn up, so dividing those by eighty per cent of your estimate keeps you out of the red. Decide in advance where any surplus goes and you avoid refunds as well.

**Q. Fewer people carry cash, and collection is scattered across methods.**
Pick one method and treat everything else as an exception. Cash only for people who arrived too recently to have a local payment app means checking two places rather than five. Past three, an unpaid share always slips through.

**Q. Some costs are reimbursed by an employer. How do we handle that?**
Keep the reimbursed portion in a separate record from the members' contributions. The company claim and the community's money have different approvers and different deadlines. Merged into one, neither is easy to explain.

**Q. Is it normal to comp the organiser's ticket?**
It varies by community. What doesn't vary is that you should say so in the invitation. Comping it silently leaves the next organiser unsure whether they're allowed to do the same.
