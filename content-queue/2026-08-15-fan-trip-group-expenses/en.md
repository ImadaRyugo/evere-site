Travelling for a show scatters payments across a group more than almost any other trip. Whoever's presale code worked pays for all the tickets, somebody else books the room, and at the venue one person queues while everyone else holds the spot.

The costs are also different in kind. **Tickets and the hotel get divided evenly; merch is entirely individual.** Leave both sitting in the same pile of "someone fronted this" and the settlement stalls the moment you try to do it.

## 🎤 Whoever got the tickets carries everyone

The person whose booking went through pays for the whole group, which puts a few hundred on their card in one go. That happens months before the show, so it's routinely forgotten by the time you're actually standing outside the venue.

Ticket cost divides cleanly. The exception is tiered seating: if two of you are in the pit and one is up in the balcony, use the actual price of each seat rather than an even split.

The real problem is the delay. **Months pass between payment and the event, and the record dies in that gap.** Write the amount down the day the booking confirms and there's nothing to remember later.

## 🏨 The room and the travel come from different people

Accommodation often gets booked before the tickets are confirmed, on the assumption that you'll cancel if the presale doesn't come through.

That means the person who booked and the people who eventually sleep there aren't always the same. Room counts change, someone drops out, someone joins. Record only the final state of the booking. Logging every revision along the way makes it harder to follow, not easier.

Travel is usually arranged individually, so it stays out of the settlement. Taxis at the other end and station lockers are shared, though, and they're worth entering even at small amounts, purely so nobody has to ask who covered them.

## 🛍️ Merch is what actually breaks the maths

If a gig trip settlement gets messy, merch is almost always why. One person queues and buys for the group, and what's in the bag differs wildly per person.

Do not split this evenly. **Since everyone bought different things, the only correct approach is per-person amounts.** One of you got a keyring, another got the tour hoodie and the vinyl. An even split makes the person who bought least subsidise the person who bought most.

Tracking it accurately in the moment is unrealistic, so either note it before you reach the counter or photograph the receipt immediately after. Blind-box items make this worse, because trades scramble who ended up with what. Lock in the amounts people paid first and treat the swapping afterwards as an exchange of objects, not of money.

## 💴 Who absorbs the booking fees

Tickets come with per-ticket booking and delivery fees. They're small enough to ignore, but anything charged per ticket was incurred on everyone's behalf and belongs in the shared pot.

A fan club subscription is different. That's an annual cost the member pays regardless, so it doesn't enter the split. Blur the two and the argument becomes that being a member is a financial penalty, at which point nobody volunteers to handle the booking next time.

## 🧮 A three-person example

Alex pays $270 for three tickets, Bo books a $210 hotel night, and Chris spends $195 at the merch stand. Of that merch, $40 is Alex's, $65 is Bo's and $90 is Chris's.

| | Tickets | Hotel | Merch | Owes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alex | $90 | $70 | $40 | $200 |
| Bo | $90 | $70 | $65 | $225 |
| Chris | $90 | $70 | $90 | $250 |

That totals $675, matching 270 + 210 + 195. Tickets and hotel are even; merch alone is per-person.

Against what they fronted — $270, $210 and $195 — Alex receives $70 while Bo pays $15 and Chris pays $55. Chris laid out $195 and still owes money, which is correct: he bought the most for himself.

## ❓ Common questions

**One of us didn't get a ticket. Do they still pay for the room?**
Not if they aren't going. If you're past the free-cancellation date the room still costs something, so decide when you book who absorbs that. Plenty of groups agree up front that an unlucky member's share gets covered by everyone, and it works well.

**Travel costs vary a lot between us. Should we even that out?**
Different starting points mean different fares, and paying your own is the sensible default. Only the legs you actually travel together belong in the shared pot. Trying to level the whole thing turns into a subsidy for whoever lives furthest away, which is much harder to get agreement on.

**The person who fronted the money says to forget it.**
Record the amount anyway. Whoever secured the tickets also put in the effort, so the rest of the group often repays them in merch or dinner, and a record makes that round trip visible. Without one, the two sides end up with different memories of what was settled.

**Someone pulls out on the day. Who eats the ticket?**
They do. If the ticket can be transferred or resold, try that first and let them cover whatever's left. Saying this out loud at the point of booking keeps it from feeling personal when it happens.
