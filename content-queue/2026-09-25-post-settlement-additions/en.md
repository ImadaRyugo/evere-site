You told everyone the figures, collected, and marked it settled. The next day a receipt turns up.

Thirty-two dollars, four ways, eight dollars each. Right there you have to decide whether to **message everyone and redo it, or let it go**.

## 🔁 A missed expense isn't an accident

There's a premise worth changing first. Missed expenses are not something care prevents.

The person who paid isn't always the person recording. Card notifications land the following day. Whoever did the supermarket run genuinely forgets they paid for it. **The moment you settle and the moment every expense is final are not the same moment.**

So deciding in advance what happens when one surfaces beats trying to make sure none does. In practice the handling matters more to the relationship than the omission ever did.

## ⚖️ Redo it, or carry it forward

Two axes decide it: the amount per person, and whether this group continues.

| Situation | Small per person | Large per person |
|---|---|---|
| You'll meet again | Carry it to next time | Redo it |
| You won't | Close it out now | Redo it |

The money threshold is whatever everybody would agree is worth the interruption. Eight dollars each justifies a message. Eighty cents each costs more in attention than it recovers.

Whether the group continues matters because a future occasion can absorb it. If you settle monthly, as people living together do, it simply joins next month. Running things as in [a monthly settlement day stops the arguments](/en/blog/monthly-settlement-day/) makes this decision mostly disappear.

## ⏱️ Late enough, and it stops being about money

Raising a missed expense is simple in proportion to how fast you do it.

The next day, "a receipt turned up" covers it. A week later, still fine. A month later the reaction changes, because the question of why now arrives ahead of the question of how much.

What the other person is weighing at that point isn't whether the figure is fair. It's whether you forgot, found it awkward, or are sitting on other things too. A conversation about money becomes a conversation about record-keeping and candour.

Which makes the time spent deciding whether to mention it the actual cost. **If you're unsure, saying it while you're still unsure is the cheapest version.**

## 📌 If you defer it, record the deferral

Where people go wrong after deciding to carry something forward is simply forgetting it.

Record the expense at the moment you decide to defer. In Evere, [log it and pick the participants](/en/docs/expenses/) and it stays unsettled, waiting for the next calculation. Leave it unmarked and it appears automatically the next time you open the settlement screen.

The important half is telling the others you've deferred it. Carry it forward privately and it arrives as brand new information at the next settlement. **A carried balance only works if it's carried by everyone who owes it.**

## 🚪 With people you won't see again, close it now

The group assembled for one trip, the colleague leaving, the project winding up. Where there is no next occasion, carrying forward isn't on the menu.

The decision collapses to two options: redo it, or write it off. And if you write it off, say so. "One more came in afterwards, I've covered it" ends the matter completely.

Write it off silently and the amount goes while the memory stays. The other person never knew, so you're the only one carrying a faint sense of having lost out. A relationship with no next time is exactly the one worth ending in words. What a settled record is actually for is covered in [why marking a debt settled matters](/en/blog/settled-record-matters/).

## ❓ Common questions

**Q. The missed expense was money I fronted. Should I still raise it?**
Yes. It looks like only you lose by staying quiet, but it resurfaces later as a discrepancy in the records. Whether to write off the money and whether to record it are separate decisions.

**Q. One person is unreachable.**
Settle with everyone else first. Waiting for a full set lets the memories of the people who are present fade too. Add that one share whenever they reappear.

**Q. The extra is so small the transfer fee exceeds it.**
Carry it forward, or take it back in kind. Paying a fee to send an amount that small makes the recipient feel awkward about accepting it.

**Q. We keep finding missed expenses.**
You probably have more than one person recording. Either pick one person to log everything, or have everybody log at the point of paying. The half-and-half version leaks the most.
