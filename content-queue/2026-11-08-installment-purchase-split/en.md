Moving in with a partner or a roommate brings big shared purchases. A fridge, a washing machine, a sofa. The amounts are heavy to pay in one go, so one of you puts it on a card installment plan or a buy now, pay later service.

That makes the split a step more complicated than usual. **An installment plan can add fees on top of the price, and the payments run for months.** If you don't agree early on who covers what, every monthly charge brings a small snag.

## Why you chose installments decides who owns the fees

Who carries the fees depends on why you went with installments in the first place.

If neither of you could pay all at once and you chose installments for both your sakes, the fees are a shared cost. Split them in the same proportion as the price.

If the cardholder chose installments for their own reasons, it is different. Say the other person could pay their half right away, but the cardholder wanted to keep cash on hand. Then the other person pays half the price, and the fees naturally stay with the cardholder.

And if installments were chosen because the other person could not pay at once, that person's share of the fees is theirs. A one-line check when you decide to spread the payments is all it takes.

## Split each monthly charge, or split the price once?

Compare the two with a fridge bought for $1,800 on A's card, over 12 months, with $120 in fees in total.

| Method | What B pays | When | Who carries the fees |
|---|---|---|---|
| Half of each monthly charge | $80 × 12 | Every month, with the charge | Shared half and half |
| Half the price, once | $900 × 1 | All at once, the month you buy | A |

The total comes to $1,920, or $160 a month. Splitting each charge means B pays $960 in total, including half the fees, $60. Paying half the price at once means B is done at $900, and the fees are treated as A's own choice.

**Splitting monthly means twelve records and twelve settle-ups.** If you already settle living costs on a fixed day each month, that is no burden. If you don't, splitting once is much harder to forget. How to set up that monthly day is in [why you need a monthly settlement day](/en/blog/monthly-settlement-day/).

## With pay later, record the purchase date, not the payment date

Buying something shared through a pay later service works the same way. The difference is the date you record.

With pay later, the day you buy and the day money leaves your account are different. It is tempting to use the payment date, but **record the split on the day of purchase.** Use the payment date and last month's shopping lands in next month's living costs, and your monthly settle-ups stop lining up.

Depending on how you choose to pay it off, some services charge a fee. If the cardholder or account holder picked that option for themselves, keeping the fee on their side is the cleanest call.

## If you stop sharing it before it's paid off

The hardest case with installments is when life changes before the payments end.

Say you split up after five of the twelve payments on that fridge. If you were splitting each charge, seven payments remain, $1,120, still running on A's card.

First decide who keeps the fridge. If A keeps it, A carries the remaining payments. If B keeps it, the payments would keep coming from A's card while B uses the fridge, so it is safer for B to pay off the remainder in one go.

How to treat the $400 B paid up to that point depends on what the fridge is worth now. Why you don't divide by the purchase price, and how to calculate it, is in [splitting up: how to divide furniture, deposits and bills](/en/blog/cohabitation-breakup-settlement/).

## Common questions

**Q. If the installment plan has no fees, does the method matter?**
The total equals the price, so neither person pays more either way. Only the timing differs, so choose based on what the other person can comfortably pay.

**Q. Only the cardholder earns points on the installment payments.**
Both of you pay for it, but the points go to one person. Whether that matters depends on the amount; the thinking is the same as in [is it unfair that the organiser keeps the card points?](/en/blog/organizer-points-debate/)

**Q. What about deferring part of the payment to a later month?**
Same logic as installments: are there fees, and whose choice was it. Payments bunch up in particular months, so agree in advance when the other person pays their share.

**Q. How should we record it in Evere?**
If you split monthly, record each charge as it comes, like "Fridge (3/12)." If you split once, record the price as one payment on the day you buy, with an [Equal](/en/docs/expenses/) split, and you're done.
