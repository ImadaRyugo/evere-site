While the band is still loading out, someone gets called over to the door. How many tickets came in tonight, how far short of the guarantee, how much is owed. The person who pays on the spot is usually one member, often whoever booked the show.

The next gig gets discussed immediately. This shortfall does not. **The gap on a ticket guarantee is the kind of money that quietly stays on one member's card.**

## The shortfall comes out of one person's pocket

Some venues and promoters ask the band to guarantee a minimum: twenty tickets at $20, say, or $400 in all. If sales do not reach it, the band covers the difference.

The count is not final until the night is over, so the gap gets settled right after the show: in cash by whoever is standing at the door, or by charging a card the band left with the venue at the start of the night.

At that moment, a debt has been created inside the band. The problem is that nobody decided how it gets shared.

## Split the unsold tickets evenly, or by each member's share?

There are two basic ways.

One is to divide the shortfall by the number of members. The band is a team, and the guarantee belongs to the whole band. It is easy to calculate and easy to explain.

The other is to divide the guarantee into a per-member share, so many tickets each, and have anyone who falls short of their share cover it. The member who brought a crowd does not end up paying for the member who brought nobody.

Which fits depends on the band. If one member just moved to town and has few friends nearby, the per-member version lands entirely on them. If the same person fills the room every time, splitting evenly leaves them quietly resentful. **What matters more than the method is settling on it before the show.** Decide afterward, looking at the bill, and whoever sold the fewest tickets stays the quietest.

## A version where the big seller doesn't lose out

A four-piece band with a guarantee of 20 tickets at $20, $400 in total. Each member's share is five tickets.

| Member | Tickets sold | vs. share of 5 | Split evenly | Split by tickets short |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 9 | +4 | $20 | $0 |
| B | 5 | 0 | $20 | $0 |
| C | 2 | −3 | $20 | $30 |
| D | 0 | −5 | $20 | $50 |
| Total | 16 | | $80 | $80 |

Sixteen tickets sold, so four tickets, $80, were short. The right-hand column divides that $80 by how far each person fell short of their share: C by three, D by five, eight in all. A's four extra tickets already filled part of the gap, so A and B owe nothing.

If you instead charged everyone for every ticket short of five, C and D would pay $160 between them, twice the actual gap. **Whatever the big seller's extra tickets already covered comes off the short members' bill.** Set it up that way and you neither over-collect nor leave the top seller out of pocket.

There are middle paths too, such as splitting half evenly and half by tickets short. Whichever you choose, Evere's [manual split](/en/docs/expenses/) lets you enter each member's amount directly, so nobody has to redo the math at the venue.

## Pick one person to hold the ticket money

The other common source of friction is where presold ticket money ends up. If each member collects cash from friends ahead of time, it becomes unclear who sold how many and where that money is now.

Either have one person collect all presale money, or have whoever receives it record it on the spot. **The record of who sold how many is exactly what the next split depends on.** Without it, the "tickets sold" column in the table above stays empty.

## Common questions

**Q. Should we split rehearsal space the same way?**
Rehearsal space is a different question: split by who showed up, or bundle it by month. The same thinking as [court fees: split per session or monthly?](/en/blog/court-fee-per-session/) applies.

**Q. What if the venue pays us back for selling over the guarantee?**
Some venues return a cut of sales above the guarantee. Rather than letting it become one person's money, put it in a band fund for the next shortfall or for gear repairs. That keeps it consistent with the table above.

**Q. Can we settle the after-show drinks together with the guarantee?**
Keep them apart. Not everyone stays for drinks, so that is a separate tab split among whoever was there. The idea of treating the second stop as its own event is in [the group dinner organizer's guide](/en/blog/drinking-party-organizer-guide/).

**Q. A member is leaving and old shortfalls are still open. What now?**
If you close out after every show, this never happens. If something is still open, list it and settle before they leave. The order that makes it easiest to raise is covered in [leaving a job: settling what's still owed](/en/blog/leaving-job-settlement/).
