Asking for separate checks or saying "let's just split it" looks like a matter of taste. It isn't. The right answer changes with the situation.

Three things decide it: **how different the orders were, how many people are at the table, and whether the restaurant can actually do it**. Taking them in turn.

## 🍽️ When separate checks are right

When the orders were genuinely different.

One person ordered the $45 entrée and three cocktails, the other had a salad and a soda. Split that evenly and the person who barely ate is visibly subsidising the person who didn't hold back. That gap doesn't stay abstract, it turns into a number, and the number affects whether they say yes to the next invitation.

Small groups also favour separate checks. With two or three people the register barely slows down. You tell the server, everyone hands over a card, done.

Lunch is usually separate too. The prices are close, the meal is short, and nobody ordered anything for the table.

## 🍻 When splitting is right

When the food came out on shared plates.

A seafood platter, a couple of pizzas, a hot pot in the middle. There's no way to count who ate how many pieces, and the act of trying is what actually ruins the evening. If shared dishes were the centre of the meal, splitting matches what happened.

Set menus and drinks packages work the same way. The per-person price was fixed before anyone ordered, so splitting and paying separately produce nearly identical numbers. When the numbers match, take the faster method.

Large groups also push toward splitting, and that's the part people miss. It gets its own section.

## ⏱️ Separate checks fall apart as the group grows

Six people asking for separate checks means six transactions at the register.

Cash means counting change, cards mean six terminal cycles, mobile payments mean unlocking six phones. Even at thirty seconds each that's three minutes, and in practice it runs past five. Meanwhile other customers are queuing, a server is stuck at the till, and whoever paid first is waiting on the sidewalk.

Restaurants also say no. Plenty of places refuse to split checks during a rush, and some point-of-sale systems simply can't divide a ticket. Getting turned down after you've announced separate checks means switching to a split on the spot, doing mental arithmetic nobody prepared for.

For a big group, **settle the payment method before you walk in, or at least before you order**, and none of this happens.

## 🔀 You don't have to pick one

Splitting and separate checks aren't the only two options, and they mix.

Share the food, split the drinks among the drinkers. This works well in any group where some people don't drink. You don't need two checks for it, just pull the bar total out of the bill, divide it among the drinkers, and divide what's left among everyone.

Say the total is $360, with $120 of that on drinks, and four of the six people drank. The $120 across four is $30 each. The remaining $240 across six is $40 each. Drinkers pay $70, non-drinkers $40. Check the arithmetic: 70 × 4 + 40 × 2 = $360.

That's the whole fix for the non-drinkers-paying-full-price problem. If you bought a bottomless drinks package, pull out the package price and the same approach applies.

## 💳 Past a certain size, one payer plus settling later wins

When the conditions line up, the fastest option is neither of the first two.

**One person puts the whole bill on a card, everyone leaves, and the rest send their share afterwards.** The register handles one transaction, so the restaurant and the queue behind you are unaffected. The arithmetic can wait until you're outside, and nobody has to round to the nearest note under pressure.

This only became practical once transfers were free and instant. It used to be that "I'll get you later" quietly became "sometime", and sometimes never. Now it happens from a phone before the group splits up.

What's left is the calculation. Who owes what, how the drinks get handled, what to do about the person who arrived after the appetisers. Try that in your head and you've reproduced the chaos at the register, just in a different place.

## ❓ FAQ

**Q. If one person always pays, they collect all the card points. Is that unfair?**
Some people think so. The simple fix is rotating who fronts the bill from one occasion to the next. If it's always going to be the same person, saying up front that the rewards go toward the next round takes the objection off the table.

**Q. Do restaurants mind being asked for separate checks?**
It depends on how busy they are and how many of you there are. Six separate checks during the lunch rush is genuinely difficult for the staff. Asking when you book — "would separate checks be okay?" — lets them plan for it.

**Q. What about people who leave early?**
Including them in the settlement afterwards is more accurate than having them throw down an estimate. Leaving extra to avoid the conversation feels easy in the moment, but the person who overpaid remembers.
