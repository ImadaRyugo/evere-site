Golf looks tidier than most outings when it comes to money. At some courses everyone pays their own way in the pro shop; at others, tee times are booked and paid online in advance.

And yet, somewhere on the drive home, someone asks how much they owe. **Only the per-player charges are settled at the counter; whatever the group or the car cost is sitting on one person's card.**

## ⛳ Is it charged per player or per group?

Look at the unit of the charge before you look at the amount.

The green fee, a rider's cart fee, and whatever you order at the turn are per-player costs. If everyone pays their own at the counter, they never need to enter the split.

Per-group costs work differently. When one person books the tee time for the foursome and prepays, the whole group's green fees are sitting on that person's card. When someone flags down the beverage cart and buys a round for all four, that is a group expense too. These land on one person and have to be divided later.

Then there is the money outside the course entirely. Gas and tolls for the drive never appear on the course's receipt at all.

## 🧮 A worked example for a foursome

Four players, one car, belonging to A. A booked the tee time online and prepaid for everyone.

| Item | Amount | Whose card | How to split |
|---|---|---|---|
| Green fees incl. cart, prepaid | $340 | A | Per player: $85 each |
| Beverage cart round for four | $48 | A | Four ways: $12 |
| Gas | $24 | A | Pool as car costs |
| Tolls, both ways | $12 | A | Same |

Car costs come to $36. Split four ways including A, the driver, that is $9 each; leave A out and the other three pay $12 each.

B, C and D each owe A their $85 green fee, $12 for the drinks, plus the car share: $106 split four ways, or $109 with the driver left out. **Apart from the counter, you only need to agree on those few numbers before you leave the parking lot.**

## 🚙 Should the driver chip in for the car?

This is where groups disagree most.

The driver carries more than gas and tolls. They supply a car that fits four golf bags, do the early-morning pickups, and drive home tired, often with nobody offering to take over.

That is why plenty of groups simply say **whoever drives does not pay toward the car**. If the same person drives every time, this is the version that lasts. If the group rotates drivers, splitting four ways every time evens out over a season.

Either works. Decide on the first round and keep it. Deciding fresh every time means the driver has to ask to be left out, every time. For longer trips by rental car, see [rental car trips: should non-drivers pay the same?](/en/blog/rental-car-trip-costs/)

## 🏆 An outing's entry fee is two budgets in one

At a golf outing or a small club tournament, entry fees flow to the organizer. Those fees split into two uses: prizes, and the dinner or drinks afterward.

Prizes are bought before the day, so they are almost always an advance from the organizer's pocket. Low gross, low net, closest to the pin, longest drive. The more categories you add, the larger the share of the fee that goes to prizes.

Say up front what happens to any money left over. **If nobody decides whether it rolls into the next event or gets handed back, the organizer ends up holding everyone's change indefinitely.** For a recurring event, rolling it forward is the least work. For setting the fee itself, the math in [setting a ticket price that breaks even](/en/blog/wedding-afterparty-accounting/) carries over.

## ❓ Common questions

**Q. One of us booked and paid for the whole foursome. How do we handle that?**
Treat it as one advance covering four people. Green fees are per player, so split them evenly, and keep anything someone added at the counter on the day as their own.

**Q. Someone spent the whole round coaching a beginner. Should we pay them back somehow?**
Covering their drinks or lunch that day usually lands better than cash. Make it something easy to accept, or the next invitation gets awkward.

**Q. What about someone who only rode one way?**
Go by the leg they rode. For a one-way passenger, half the gas plus one direction of tolls is an easy line to draw.

**Q. Do we split the driving range too?**
A bucket of balls is a per-player cost, so everyone usually pays their own. Record it as an advance only when one person paid for the group.
