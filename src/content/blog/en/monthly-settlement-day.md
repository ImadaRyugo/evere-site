When couples who keep their finances separate start struggling a few months in, the split ratio is rarely the problem. You hear the same story often: hours spent deciding between 60/40 and down the middle, and it still fell apart.

The reason it falls apart is that **nobody set a day to settle**.

## 📆 What happens without one

Separate finances with no settlement day break in one of two ways.

The first is exhaustion from settling constantly. Sending $32 back every time one of you spends $64 at the grocery store doesn't survive a week. The effort is enormous relative to the amount. "I'll let this one go" starts creeping in, and then the recording stops too.

The second is letting it pile up until it's unrecoverable. Trying to reconcile six months of expenses in one sitting means missing receipts and expenses nobody can identify anymore. At that point accurate settlement is impossible. Someone says "forget it" and it ends there, but whoever had been paying more definitely remembers.

A settlement day is the device that avoids both. **Don't settle every time, but don't let it accumulate either.** Once a month, all at once.

## 🗓️ Choosing the date

It doesn't have to be the end of the month. In fact, the end of the month is usually a bad choice.

Anchor it to **the day after payday**. Putting settlement just after money lands means the transfer never bounces off a thin balance. If you're paid on the 15th and the last day of the month, use the 16th or the 1st. When the two of you are paid on different schedules, use the later one.

Once you've chosen it, put it in both calendars as a repeating event. "Whenever we remember" is functionally the same as having no settlement day.

The work itself is fixed: check the month's shared expenses, total them, transfer the difference. Once you're used to it, five minutes.

## 🏷️ Write down what counts as shared

Couples whose settlement day drags out have one thing in common. Every month, they're relitigating whether something belongs on the list.

**Decide the boundary once and write it down.** Rent, utilities, internet, groceries, household supplies. Nobody argues about those. It's the middle cases that cause trouble.

Alcohol only one of you drinks. A gift for one person's parents. A restaurant you went to together. One person's medical bills. The car. There's no correct answer for where these land, and because there's no correct answer, deciding in the moment lets whoever is more forceful win. Build the list when nothing is at stake and keep it in a shared note.

When something comes up that isn't on the list, treat it as personal for that month and decide the rule at the next settlement. Rules made mid-argument get forgotten by the person who made them.

## 🧮 Settle on the net, not item by item

The calculation on settlement day isn't paying each other back one expense at a time.

Say one of you covered $850 in groceries and utilities, the other $1,250 in rent and internet. Shared costs total $2,100, so an even split is $1,050 each. The first person sends 1,050 − 850 = $200 and it's finished. One transfer for the month.

If the split is 60/40 rather than even, the first person's share is $1,260 and the second's is $840. They paid $850, so they send $410. Checking the other side: the second person paid $1,250 against a share of $840, so they receive $410. The numbers agree.

Totalling first and transferring the difference is what's meant by settling on the **net**. Thirty expenses still come down to a single transfer. [Cutting the number of transfers](/en/blog/minimize-settlements/) goes into the mechanics.

## 📱 Grind it down to the same five minutes

A settlement routine should get shorter the longer you run it.

What has to shrink is **the work you do on the day itself**. If settlement day means digging out receipts and adding them up, it takes thirty minutes a month and you will eventually stop. Record expenses as they happen and the day is reduced to confirming a number and sending money.

Wherever you keep the record, both of you need to be able to see the same thing. A note on one person's phone is invisible to the other. What can't be seen doesn't get trusted, and a record that isn't trusted gets re-checked at every settlement.

In Evere, that's one shared [project](/en/docs/projects/) that you both add expenses to. If you're not splitting evenly, set a [ratio split](/en/docs/expenses/) and the shares calculate themselves. On settlement day you open the [settlement](/en/docs/settlement/) screen, check the figure, transfer it, mark it settled, and the month is closed.

Couples who make separate finances work aren't the ones with a cleverer ratio. They're the ones doing the same short thing on the same day every month.
